The Case for Change

ECS Is Not Broken.
Its Architecture Is.

ECS was engineered for a specific job: close fast, cover subsidiary ground, stay below the Core AE ceiling. It did that job well — and still does. What changed isn't the talent. It's the commercial landscape.

Salesforce's portfolio has shifted to consumption. Agentforce and Data Cloud close as credits — and those credits need to be activated to protect NRR. ECS covers the accounts at the front line of that activation challenge. Its architecture wasn't built for it.

The proposal is additive — built on top of what ECS does today, not a replacement for it. ECS already has the accounts. The architecture update is what unlocks the next layer of value.

Part 2 · The Motion

Add AER to What ECS Already Does

Create & Close doesn't go away — it becomes the Expand pillar. Activate and Retain are net-new motions layered on top. ECS is already in the accounts. The model adds purpose to what those relationships can accomplish.

Business Outcome 1
Drives cross-cloud platform selling
ECS AEs who own an Activation Book are incentivized to get Agentforce and Data Cloud deployed — which creates the natural pull for adjacent cloud expansion.
Business Outcome 2
Multi-cloud stickiness protects NRR
Customers who activate on multiple clouds churn at a fraction of the rate of single-cloud customers. Consumption activation is retention insurance.
Business Outcome 3
Simplifies the customer experience
ECS is already in the account — no new team, no new relationship, no new friction. The customer gets a single motion from a rep they already trust.

ECS Consumption Activation (ECA) Program

Each ECS AE is assigned an Activation Book — a live dashboard of all unactivated Agentforce and Data Cloud credits across their accounts. Quarterly compensation is tied to % of book at active burn threshold. Activation milestones unlock a quarterly bonus on top of standard ACV attainment.

Rapid Deployment Toolkit

Pre-built use case libraries by vertical (HLS HIPAA agents, FINS compliance, CBS member services), a curated ISV partner network for fast stand-up, and connection to a dedicated Agentforce SE pod for accounts above an activation threshold. ECS brings the relationship. The toolkit delivers the first deployment.

The Hyperscaler Playbook

AWS and Azure have proven this model: embedding account-intimate reps with a post-sale activation mandate dramatically improves credit burn rates and NRR. Salesforce's ECS org has all the account relationships needed to replicate this. The only missing piece is the compensation structure and toolkit.

A motion without a career path is a sprint, not a strategy. The AEs who run AER best will leave within 18 months if ECS can't give them a reason to stay. Part 3 makes the case for L7 and L8 — and defines exactly what earning them requires.

L8 — The Account Intelligence Officer

What "Account Intelligence" Means in Practice

🗺️

Live Account Activation Map

L8 AEs maintain a structured map of each Enterprise parent account: product footprint by subsidiary and BU, activation status of all consumption products, executive contact map, and competitive exposure signals. Refreshed quarterly. Shared with Core AE leadership.

📋

Exec QBR Co-Presentation

L8 AEs co-present at quarterly business reviews with Core AEs, presenting the subsidiary/consumption view of the account. This positions ECS as a strategic partner — not an execution layer — and builds the executive relationships that protect the account long-term.

🔍

Early Warning System

ECS AEs are closest to the day-to-day account footprint — the first to spot at-risk signals months before renewal. L8 AEs formalize this into structured quarterly at-risk reporting to Core AE leadership. Retention saves credited to NRR contribution score.

The "paper pusher" narrative dies here. You cannot paper-push your way to an Account Intelligence briefing that an SVP relies on before a QBR. The work changes the perception — not a rebrand.

Career architecture solves the talent problem. But there's a second structural ceiling in the way — the deal band itself. Part 4 shows why $150K no longer reflects the commercial reality of the accounts ECS already owns.

Part 4 · The Commercial Reality Gap

Enterprise Transactional Business Has No Owner — ECS Is Working It Through Workarounds

When Enterprise accounts run Agentforce activations, expand Data Cloud, or upgrade subsidiary platforms, the natural order size clusters between $150K and $280K. Not a strategic transformation. Not a Core deal. Just the transactional volume that flows from a mature Enterprise relationship. That business has no clean structural home today — and ECS is already working it through split orders, ramps, and Deal Desk engineering.

1
The Volume Math That Nobody Discusses
An ECS AE carrying a $1M–$2M annual quota cannot close their number on deals averaging sub-$150K. The subsidiary account base doesn't produce enough deal volume to support it. This isn't a performance problem — it's an arithmetic problem. The architecture was never calibrated to make the quota math work, and the field has been absorbing that gap quietly for years.
Sub-$150K deal size · Quota math that doesn't close · Field absorbing the gap silently
2
Where Enterprise Transactional Business Actually Lives
Agentforce activations, consumption expansions, and subsidiary platform upgrades for Enterprise accounts naturally produce orders in the $150K–$280K range. This isn't a big deal — it's routine transactional business. It doesn't require Core. It doesn't require a strategic transformation motion. It requires the AE who already owns the relationship and knows what the account has bought. That's ECS. There is no structural reason for this business to flow anywhere else.
$150K–$280K transactional Enterprise business · Relationship already owned by ECS · No clean structural home
3
The Workaround Culture Is the Evidence
Split orders across fiscal periods. Activation phases spread across quarters. Single-product line items that should be one order routed as multiples through Deal Desk. These aren't creative deal structures — they're symptoms. ECS AEs are engineering ACV to fit a ceiling that doesn't match the market. Each workaround generates 2–3 Deal Desk touches, a legal review, and a renewal complexity event. Core AEs have noticed the pattern and route around ECS on accounts where they've seen it. The workaround culture doesn't go away until the architecture changes.

The Quota Math Has Never Worked at Sub-$150K

ECS AEs can't close $1M+ annual quotas on a diet of sub-$150K deals. The account base doesn't produce enough volume. This has been true for years. The field knows it. The architecture hasn't responded to it.

$150K–$280K Is Transactional, Not Strategic

Deals in this range aren't complex enough to require Core. They're the natural output of a healthy ECS relationship doing its job. They belong with the AE who owns the account — not in a structural gray zone where workarounds are required to get them closed.

Structural Ownership Ends the Workaround Culture

When transactional Enterprise business has a defined home, split orders stop. Deal Desk load drops. Core AEs stop routing around ECS on accounts where they've seen ceiling-engineering. Clean ownership is a field partnership unlock — not just a comp plan adjustment.

The workaround culture has a dollar figure attached to it. Every split order, every Deal Desk routing, every artificially-phased activation carries a cost — in time, in renewal complexity, and in field relationships. Part 5 puts a number on it.
