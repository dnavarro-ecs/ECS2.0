The Case for Change

ECS Is Not Broken.
Its Architecture Is.

ECS was engineered for a specific job: move fast, close small, and cover the subsidiary ground that Core AEs couldn't. It did that job exceptionally well — and still does. What changed isn't ECS's talent or work ethic. It's the commercial landscape the org now operates inside.

Salesforce's portfolio has shifted to consumption. Agentforce and Data Cloud close as credits — and those credits need to be activated to generate the expansion and retention that protects NRR at the board level. The subsidiaries ECS covers are the front line of that activation challenge. But ECS's structure, compensation model, and grade architecture were all designed before that frontier existed.

This readout names three core tensions — and proposes the structural changes to resolve them.

Tension 1 · Compensation
Consumption is live. ECS isn't wired to it.
Agentforce and Data Cloud credits go unactivated inside ECS accounts. Zero compensation exists for the team best positioned to change that.
Tension 2 · Talent
ECS grows talent — then exports it.
L6 is the end of the road. High performers hit the ceiling, do the math, and leave — taking 3–5 years of account knowledge with them.
Tension 3 · Structure
The deal ceiling creates the friction it was meant to prevent.
When the ceiling is below commercial reality, reps split orders and stagger activations — generating the field distrust that damages ECS's reputation.

Part 2 · The New Motion

Replace Create & Close with AER

Activate. Expand. Retain. Three distinct motions that together cover the full revenue lifecycle inside Enterprise accounts — and align ECS directly with where Salesforce's business is going.

A
Activate
Convert purchased but unused Agentforce and Data Cloud credits into live, measurable deployments. ECS AEs own an "Activation Book" — the total unactivated consumption across their account set. This is the consumption activation play Salesforce has no one running today.
Key Metrics
› % of credit book at active burn threshold
› # of Agentforce use cases deployed per quarter
› Activation ACV credit (new compensation component)
E
Expand
The evolved BCO motion — identify and close new whitespace across subsidiaries, new divisions, and adjacent products. C&C is retained and elevated within this pillar. Deal ceiling is raised to eliminate ACV Engineering and allow clean, commercial deals at appropriate size.
Key Metrics
› BCO ACV (primary attainment, as today)
› Pipeline generation participation rate
› % ECS Own on sourced pipeline
R
Retain
Proactive early engagement on at-risk renewals within the ECS coverage zone. ECS AEs are the first to spot compliance issues, org changes, exec turnover, and budget pressure — often months before Core AEs engage. Formalizing this as a KPI connects ECS to NRR, the metric that matters most to Salesforce leadership.
Key Metrics
› NRR contribution score (renewals where ECS engaged early)
› At-risk flags surfaced per quarter
› Renewal save rate on ECS-flagged accounts

Why AER beats C&C: Create & Close optimizes for deal velocity — which produces short-cycle, small deals and discourages any play that doesn't close in-quarter. AER optimizes for revenue quality — activation rate, expansion rate, and NRR contribution. These are the metrics Salesforce leadership reports to Wall Street. Connecting ECS performance to those metrics changes ECS's strategic position in the company.

ECS Consumption Activation (ECA) Program

Each ECS AE is assigned an Activation Book — a live dashboard of all unactivated Agentforce and Data Cloud credits across their accounts. Quarterly compensation is tied to % of book at active burn threshold. Activation milestones unlock a quarterly bonus on top of standard ACV attainment.

Rapid Deployment Toolkit

Pre-built use case libraries by vertical (HLS HIPAA agents, FINS compliance, CBS member services), a curated ISV partner network for fast stand-up, and connection to a dedicated Agentforce SE pod for accounts above an activation threshold. ECS brings the relationship. The toolkit delivers the first deployment.

The Hyperscaler Playbook

AWS and Azure have proven this model: embedding account-intimate reps with a post-sale activation mandate dramatically improves credit burn rates and NRR. Salesforce's ECS org has all the account relationships needed to replicate this. The only missing piece is the compensation structure and toolkit.

L8 — The Account Intelligence Officer

What "Account Intelligence" Means in Practice

🗺️

Live Account Activation Map

L8 AEs maintain a structured map of each Enterprise parent account: product footprint by subsidiary and BU, activation status of all consumption products, executive contact map, and competitive exposure signals. Refreshed quarterly. Shared with Core AE leadership.

📋

Exec QBR Co-Presentation

L8 AEs co-present at quarterly business reviews with Core AEs, presenting the subsidiary/consumption view of the account. This positions ECS as a strategic partner — not an execution layer — and builds the executive relationships that protect the account long-term.

🔍

Early Warning System

ECS AEs are closest to the day-to-day account footprint — the first to spot at-risk signals months before renewal. L8 AEs formalize this into structured quarterly at-risk reporting to Core AE leadership. Retention saves credited to NRR contribution score.

The "paper pusher" narrative dies here. You cannot paper-push your way to an Account Intelligence briefing that an Enterprise SVP relies on before a QBR. The role change — from deal executor to account intelligence officer — is what changes the field's perception. Not messaging. Not a rebrand. The work itself.

Part 4 · The ACV Engineering Problem

Deal Band Friction Is the #1 ECS / Field Trust Issue

When deal ceilings are set below the natural commercial size of the account, reps don't stop selling — they engineer around the ceiling. This destroys deal integrity, creates contractual complexity, and poisons the ECS/Field relationship.

1
The Account Has a $280K Opportunity
A subsidiary needs a platform upgrade + add-on cloud worth $280K ACV. Clean, logical commercial deal. ECS AE identifies it, builds the case, aligns procurement. This is exactly what ECS should be doing.
$280K natural deal size
2
Old Ceiling Hits — ACV Engineering Begins
At $150K (old ceiling), the ECS AE splits the order: Order 1 is $149K, Order 2 is $131K with a staggered activation date. This satisfies the threshold rule on paper while delivering the full commercial value — but at a cost: two order forms, two amendment processes, procurement confusion, and a Core AE who now has a contractual mess in their account.
2 order forms · Procurement friction · Field relationship damage
3
The Real Cost of ACV Engineering
It is not just a paperwork issue. Split orders require double the Deal Desk touches, double the legal review, and create co-term complexity that haunts the account at renewal. Core AEs who inherit these structures learn to route around ECS. Field trust erodes. The very account intimacy ECS has built is undermined by the structural rule forcing the behavior.
Damaged field relationships · Double Deal Desk load · Renewal complexity
4
The Fix: A Meaningful Deal Ceiling Increase
Raising the deal ceiling to $350K eliminates the engineering incentive for the vast majority of ECS deals. The AE who owns the account closes the deal — cleanly, as a single order. Most natural commercial opportunities in Enterprise subsidiaries fall below $350K. Deals that genuinely exceed that threshold have earned escalation to Core, which is correct and healthy. As AEs grow into L7 and L8 roles, their individual ceiling grows with them — not as a routing rule, but as a reflection of the account complexity and deal size those roles are built for.
One clean order · Less Deal Desk friction · Stronger field partnership

Why the Current Ceiling Breaks Field Trust

Core AEs see split order forms and staggered activations and correctly identify them as structural workarounds. Worse — they get pulled into $160K transactional deals they shouldn't be anywhere near. Field AEs should be driving transformation. Every minute spent on a transactional escalation is time not spent on the $5M strategic opportunity in the same parent account.

$350K Is the Baseline, Not a Stretch Goal

The natural commercial size of most ECS subsidiary deals — platform upgrades, consumption expansions, renewal uplifts — clusters between $150K and $350K. The current $150K ceiling doesn't sit below that range. It sits in the middle of it. $350K isn't an aggressive ask. It's the ceiling that reflects commercial reality.

Ceiling + Grade Architecture = Clean Ownership

As AEs grow into L7 and L8 roles, their ceiling grows with them — reflecting the account complexity those roles are built for. An L7 owns their accounts fully up to $350K. An L8 up to $500K+. Escalation to Core happens when a deal genuinely exceeds what ECS should own — not because an arbitrary ceiling forces engineering at $150K.

Part 5 · The Business Case

The Cost of Inaction Is Not Gradual — It Is Compounding

Three quantifiable levers. Each one independently justifies the investment. Together, they make the case that ECS 2.0 is not a compensation negotiation — it is a revenue transformation.

1
Lever 1: Agentforce / Data Cloud Activation Revenue
Salesforce is closing Agentforce and Data Cloud deals across AMER Enterprise accounts. The dollar figure on unactivated credits across the ECS account base is obtainable from CRM in under an hour — it is the single strongest anchor this business case can carry. The methodology: pull all Agentforce / Data Cloud contracts closed against accounts in the Patrick Reid org, flag those with activation rates below burn-rate threshold, and sum the unactivated ACV. Industry benchmarks (AWS, Azure) consistently show 30–40% of contracted consumption goes unactivated in year one. If ECS accounts track at that rate, the unactivated credit book across 83 AEs likely sits in the tens of millions. Even a 20-point improvement in activation rate — a modest target given ECS's account intimacy — represents a direct ARR protection event on each of those deals.
Target data pull: unactivated credit book across Patrick Reid org accounts → this becomes the headline number
2
Lever 2: Senior ECS AE Attrition Cost
Replacing a tenured ECS AE costs 6–12 months of recruiting plus 6–12 months of ramp. During that window the account set underperforms — deals are missed, activation stalls, field relationships cool. Industry benchmarks for quota-carrying AE replacement put loaded cost at $150K–$200K in direct spend before accounting for the ACV gap during ramp. The precise number for this org is verifiable: FY25 and FY26 ECS attrition data and post-departure account performance are available from HR and Sales Ops. That pull transforms this from an industry estimate into a provable figure. Even at the conservative end — two exits per year, $150K replacement cost — that is $300K annually in avoidable spend. The retention investment required to create L7/L8 is a fraction of that. The case holds at any plausible attrition rate.
Verify: pull FY25/FY26 ECS attrition data from HR → convert estimate to proof
3
Lever 3: ACV Engineering Elimination
Every split order form is a Deal Desk touch, a legal review, and a renewal complexity event. The Deal Desk time alone on ACV-engineered deals represents a measurable drag on deal ops capacity. Beyond the internal cost, the field trust damage from split orders creates a collaboration deficit that costs ECS deal flow — Core AEs route around ECS on accounts where they've seen engineering behavior. Raising the ceiling to eliminate this behavior pays back in Deal Desk efficiency, field collaboration, and deal quality.
