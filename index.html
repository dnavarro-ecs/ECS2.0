The Case for Change

ECS Is Not Broken.
Its Architecture Is.

ECS was engineered for a specific job: move fast, close small, and cover the subsidiary ground that Core AEs couldn't. It did that job exceptionally well — and still does. What changed isn't ECS's talent or work ethic. It's the commercial landscape the org now operates inside.

Salesforce's portfolio has shifted to consumption. Agentforce and Data Cloud close as credits — and those credits need to be activated to generate the expansion and retention that protects NRR at the board level. The subsidiaries ECS covers are the front line of that activation challenge. But ECS's structure, compensation model, and grade architecture were all designed before that frontier existed.

The proposal is additive — built on top of what ECS does today, not a replacement for it. ECS already has the accounts. The architecture update is what unlocks the next layer of value.

Part 2 · The Motion

Add AER to What ECS Already Does

Create & Close doesn't go away — it becomes the Expand pillar. Activate and Retain are net-new motions layered on top. ECS is already in the accounts. The model adds purpose to what those relationships can accomplish.

Business Outcome 1
Drives cross-cloud platform selling
ECS AEs who own an Activation Book are incentivized to get Agentforce and Data Cloud deployed — which creates the natural pull for adjacent cloud expansion.
Business Outcome 2
Multi-cloud stickiness protects NRR
Customers who activate on multiple clouds churn at a fraction of the rate of single-cloud customers. Consumption activation is retention insurance.
Business Outcome 3
Simplifies the customer experience
ECS is already in the account — no new team, no new relationship, no new friction. The customer gets a single motion from a rep they already trust.

Connecting ECS performance to those metrics changes ECS's strategic position in the company.

ECS Consumption Activation (ECA) Program

Each ECS AE is assigned an Activation Book — a live dashboard of all unactivated Agentforce and Data Cloud credits across their accounts. Quarterly compensation is tied to % of book at active burn threshold. Activation milestones unlock a quarterly bonus on top of standard ACV attainment.

Pre-built use case libraries by vertical (HLS HIPAA agents, FINS compliance, CBS member services), a curated ISV partner network for fast stand-up, and connection to a dedicated Agentforce SE pod for accounts above an activation threshold. Salesforce's ECS org has all the account relationships needed to replicate this. The only missing piece is the compensation structure and toolkit.

The Ceiling Was Set for a Different Product

When $150K was established as the ECS deal ceiling, the commercial range of a subsidiary deal was structured around BCO and license expansion. Agentforce, Data Cloud, and multi-cloud consumption had not yet become the primary commercial motion. The ceiling made sense for the product it was built for. It doesn't reflect what ECS is selling today.

$350K Is the Baseline, Not a Stretch Goal

The natural commercial size of most ECS subsidiary deals in FY27 — platform upgrades, consumption activations, renewal uplifts — clusters between $150K and $350K. That makes $150K the floor of the commercial range, not the ceiling. $350K isn't an aggressive ask. It's the number that reflects where ECS accounts actually sit today.

Ceiling + Grade Architecture = Clean Ownership

As AEs grow into L7 and L8 roles, their ceiling grows with them — reflecting the account complexity those roles are built for. An L7 owns their accounts fully up to $350K. An L8 up to $500K+. Escalation to Core happens when a deal genuinely exceeds what ECS should own — creating clear boundaries at every level, not contested hand-offs.

Part 6 · Honest Pressure-Testing

What This Model Has to Earn

Every structural change has landmines. These are the five most likely objections — and the response to each. A proposal that can't answer these isn't ready for a comp committee.

⚠️

Risk: Activation focus dilutes in-quarter ACV creation

If AEs are chasing activation bonuses, Create & Close velocity may dip during the transition. This is the most credible objection a sales leader will raise.

Response: AER is structured so Expand (BCO ACV) remains the primary attainment metric. Activation and Retain are additive comp levers — they can't replace quota attainment, only supplement it. The pilot phase tracks both in parallel to catch any regression early.
⚠️

Risk: L7/L8 grades set a precedent across all AE orgs

The comp committee's first concern will be: if ECS gets L7/L8, every segment will ask for it. Grade creation has org-wide implications.

Response: L7/L8 eligibility is gated on ECS-specific criteria: multi-cloud account tenure, activation book ownership, and executive engagement requirements. These are not portable to other AE orgs without the same account model. The job profiles must make this explicit before submission.
⚠️

Risk: A more empowered ECS encroaches on Core AE territory

Higher deal ceilings and a broader motion could be perceived by Core AEs as competitive — creating the exact friction this model is meant to eliminate.

Response: The ceiling expansion applies to deals ECS already owns — it doesn't change account assignment. The Retain motion is explicitly collaborative: ECS flags at-risk accounts to Core, not around them. Rules of engagement need to be written before launch.
⚠️

Risk: Activation metrics don't exist in CRM today

The Activation Book concept requires infrastructure — credit burn tracking, deployment milestones, attribution logic — that Sales Ops would need to build. This is non-trivial.

Response: This is a real dependency and a real timeline risk. The roadmap accounts for it: the Activation Book dashboard is a Q4 FY27 deliverable, and the pilot doesn't launch until the measurement infrastructure is in place. Rushing this creates the wrong incentive.
⚠️

Risk: Digital already exists for consumption activation — why AER?

Reid runs both ECS and Digital. Stephen Harrison's Digital org was explicitly designed for consumption-based revenue motions. A comp committee will ask: isn't this what Digital is for?

Response: Digital AEs don't have the day-to-day account presence inside Enterprise subsidiaries that ECS has. AER works precisely because ECS is already in the room — it's an elevation of the existing coverage model, not a new overlay. Digital and AER are complementary layers, not competing motions. Reid built the portfolio to interlock, not to consolidate.
The Honest Summary
This model works if two things are true: ECS AEs can hold both a transactional and a consultative motion simultaneously, and Salesforce's ops infrastructure can measure activation in a way that's clean enough to compensate against. Both are answerable — but they have to be answered before the comp committee meeting, not after it.
